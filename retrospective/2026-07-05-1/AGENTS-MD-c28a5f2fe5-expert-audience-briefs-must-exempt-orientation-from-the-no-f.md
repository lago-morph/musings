# agent instruction

**Expert-audience briefs must exempt orientation from the no-filler rule.** "When writing an authoring brief for expert-audience content, never let "respect the reader / cut filler / earn every sentence" stand unqualified. State explicitly that openings, plain-language gists, motivation, and connective tissue are NOT filler and are mandatory — expertise in adjacent fields does not substitute for orientation in the new material."

*Grounded in: the reviewer calling the first 20-node fan-out "unreadable ... no entry point".*

# justification

The original voice brief told authors the reader was a PhD who needed "no hand-holding" and that every sentence must "earn its place." Eleven subagents faithfully produced nodes that opened mid-thought with formalism — and the actual reader (the same expert the brief described) judged the result worse than reading the primary papers, because papers at least have abstracts and introductions. The recovery brief made a ~150-word orientation structurally mandatory before any equation, and the same reviewer approved the result immediately. The failure was not subagent quality; it was a brief that encoded terseness as respect.
